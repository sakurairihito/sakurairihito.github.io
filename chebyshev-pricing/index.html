Chebyshev interpolation for option pricing

Precompute a price surface for fast repeated evaluation.

Calibration and risk calculations require prices at many parameter settings. Gaß, Glau, Mahlstedt and Mair (2018) approximate the dependence of the price on those parameters with Chebyshev polynomials, so the expensive pricing calculation can be reused. Original paper.

Offline and online

  1. Offline: choose a parameter box, evaluate prices at Chebyshev nodes, and construct the interpolating polynomial. The node values can come from Monte Carlo, Fourier integration, or a PDE solver.

  2. Online: evaluate this polynomial at new parameters inside the box.

After rescaling the parameters to \(p\in[-1,1]^D\), the approximation is

\[ V(p)\approx\sum_{j_1=0}^{n_1}\cdots\sum_{j_D=0}^{n_D} c_{j_1,\ldots,j_D}\prod_{i=1}^{D}T_{j_i}(p_i), \]

where \(T_j\) is a Chebyshev polynomial and \(D\) counts the varying parameters.

Why it works

A payoff may have kinks even when its expected value is analytic in the parameters. The paper gives sufficient conditions for this analyticity: interpolation errors then decrease exponentially with polynomial degree. In terms of the total number of nodes \(N\), the tensor construction gives an error of order \(\rho^{-N^{1/D}}\) for some \(\rho>1\). It also establishes convergence results for price sensitivities. Sections 2–3.

Errors in the precomputed prices also propagate through the interpolation. Increasing the degree alone cannot remove Monte Carlo noise; node accuracy and interpolation accuracy must be balanced. Section 2.3.

What Section 4 demonstrates

The experiments include basket and path-dependent options under Black–Scholes, Heston and Merton models, and an American put priced by finite differences. These extend beyond the scope of the analyticity results.

For a five-asset Heston lookback option, the efficiency study varies two model parameters, using \(7\times7=49\) interpolation nodes. At roughly comparable accuracy, the reported cost for 10,000 parameter pairs is:

MethodTime
Monte Carlo at every pairAbout 39 days, extrapolated from a single evaluation
Chebyshev interpolation, including offline constructionAbout 23 hours
Online evaluation aloneAbout 7 seconds

Including construction, the methods break even at roughly 225 price evaluations in this implementation. These timings describe the paper's particular benchmark. Section 4.2.

Connection to compression

The full grid still needs \(\prod_i(n_i+1)\) nodes: five assets do not mean five interpolated parameters. More varying parameters make tensor compression or sparse grids attractive, as discussed in the paper's outlook. Analyticity alone does not establish a small tensor rank.

The price-surface compression example illustrates the complementary roles: low polynomial degree limits detail along each axis, while low rank compresses the coupling between axes.

Two reproduced numerical notes make the distinction concrete: a spread option with Chebfun3 builds a three-parameter Tucker approximation, while interpolation across a kink compares one polynomial with two pieces joined at a known exercise boundary.

Option prices near expiry shows how a smooth Black–Scholes price approaches a kinked payoff, and why a domain extending to zero remaining maturity needs separate treatment.

Paper

Maximilian Gaß, Kathrin Glau, Mirco Mahlstedt and Maximilian Mair, Chebyshev interpolation for parametric option pricing, Finance and Stochastics 22, 701–731 (2018).
